Earnings Alpha

An NLP-driven quantitative research pipeline that extracts sentiment signals from earnings call transcripts and tests whether they predict short-term abnormal stock returns.

The Question

When a company reports earnings, executives get on a call and talk about the business. Most analysts focus on the numbers - EPS, revenue, guidance. But what about the language? Does the tone of what a CEO says contain information the market hasn't fully priced in yet?

"The sentiment of earnings call transcripts combined with earnings surprise predicts short-term abnormal stock returns."

The Pipeline

An end-to-end quantitative research workflow across 5 notebooks - from raw SEC filings to a backtested long-short portfolio strategy.

01
Data Collection
Scraped 50 8-K filings from SEC EDGAR using sec-edgar-downloader and identified 20 containing earnings call transcripts through keyword matching. Pulled 2 years of daily stock prices and EPS actuals vs estimates using yfinance. Parsed transcript text using BeautifulSoup.
02
NLP Sentiment Scoring
Loaded ProsusAI/finbert - a BERT model fine-tuned on financial text - and scored each transcript using a sliding window of 480-token chunks with overlap to handle the 512-token limit. Produced sentiment scores from -1 (negative) to +1 (positive).
03
Feature Engineering
Constructed two signals: Earnings Surprise (standardized EPS beat/miss) and Tone Surprise (how much more positive or negative management sounded vs their own historical baseline). Both z-scored and combined into a Composite Alpha Signal.
04
Event Study
Downloaded Fama-French 3-factor daily data. For each event, fit a 120-day estimation window factor model (days -130 to -11) and computed Cumulative Abnormal Returns (CAR) over a [-1, +3] day event window.
05
Statistical Testing & Backtest
Ran OLS regression of CAR on the composite signal. Built a quarterly long-short portfolio - going long the top 2 signal stocks and short the bottom 2 - and evaluated against SPY using quantstats.

03

Signal Construction

The alpha signal fuses two complementary information sources - one quantitative and one linguistic - into a single composite score.

// Earnings Alpha - Composite Signal Construction EARNINGS SURPRISE Formula -> (Actual EPS - Estimated EPS) / |Estimated EPS| Source -> yfinance earnings_dates Normalize-> z-score across all events TONE SURPRISE Formula -> Current Sentiment - Mean(Prior Calls, same ticker) Source -> FinBERT on SEC 8-K prepared remarks Normalize-> z-score across all events COMPOSITE SIGNAL Combine -> (tone_surprise_z + earnings_surprise_z) / 2 Range -> continuous, centered at 0 EVENT STUDY Model -> Fama-French 3-factor (MKT-RF, SMB, HML) Estimate -> 120-day window (t-130 to t-11) Event -> CAR over [-1, +3] day window H0: composite_signal has no predictive power over CAR
Earnings Surprise
Standardized EPS beat/miss captures the quantitative information shock. Positive surprise means the company beat analyst expectations.
Tone Surprise
Measures how different management's language is from their own baseline. A CEO who sounds unusually positive relative to their own history may be signaling something the numbers alone don't capture.
Fama-French Adjustment
Abnormal returns are computed by stripping out market, size and value factor exposures - isolating the stock-specific reaction to the earnings event.
CAR [-1, +3]
The 5-day cumulative abnormal return window captures both pre-event leakage (day -1) and the multi-day price discovery process after the call.
04

Results

The composite signal explains 23% of variation in abnormal returns across 15 earnings events. Earnings surprise is marginally significant at the 10% level - a 1 standard deviation increase is associated with a 3.3% higher CAR, which is economically meaningful.

05

Honest Caveats

Intellectual honesty matters more than clean results. These are the known limitations and what they point to:

!
Small sample (n=15) - reliable OLS inference requires ~30+ observations. Results are directional, not conclusive
!
No transaction costs - real short selling incurs borrow fees and bid-ask spreads not modeled here
!
Single sector - all 5 companies are mega-cap tech. Results may not generalize across sectors or market caps
!
Q&A sections empty - the transcript splitter failed to isolate Q&A from SEC 8-K press releases. The model scores prepared remarks only
06

What I Learned

The earnings surprise signal has real predictive content even in a small sample. Tone surprise points in the right direction but requires a larger dataset to achieve conventional significance. The gap between strategy and benchmark Sharpe ratios points directly to the next steps: volatility targeting, sector neutralization and expanding the universe to S&P 500 scale.

More broadly, this project taught me that the hardest part of quantitative research is not the modeling - it is the data pipeline. Parsing messy SEC filings, aligning dates across disparate sources and handling edge cases cleanly took more time and care than any single statistical test.
